# MCST integrity watchdog — planning review

## Owner decisions superseding this discussion draft — 18 October 2026

G12 is resolved for local construction. Defaults **20% quote revision / 30% selected price above lowest / 20% cumulative variation growth / 3 offers**, inclusive equality and no SGD floor. New Contractor quotes must be positive. Estate settings apply next RFQ round; ticket overrides apply to future events only, with mandatory unchecked acknowledgments and immutable earlier parameters. Admin may edit alongside MCST. A flagged CM selection requires MCST clearance even below the monetary approval threshold; an exact flagged quote does not taint an unflagged rival. A flagged variation pauses all ordinary work until explicit clearance, including after withdrawal/rejection; factual make-safe records remain available. Clearance requires exact version, named authority, time and explanation. MCST can combine selection/approval/clearance. Both MCST/Admin receive grouped warning notices; raw protected-access logs are Admin-only, and MCST receives only an explicitly approved summary. No automated misconduct finding or reputation penalty. [Build evidence](implementation/CHAPTERS_17_18_BUILD.md) and [ADR 009](architecture/ADR_009_INTEGRITY_AND_DECISION_PACKS.md). Contrary advisory-only/open-default statements below are historical.

Status: discussion draft, 12 October 2026. This document records decisions made in the separate MCST integrity chat and labels further ideas as proposals. It does not authorise application implementation or change the lifecycle, reputation, or admin-alert policies in the shared decision register.

## Confirmed in the integrity discussion

- Confidential conduct cases are restricted by named account. A reported MCST user cannot see the case, its notifications, or its protected material even though MCST users ordinarily share estate permissions. Admin receives the case first and may involve an uninvolved MCST user as appropriate. The existing notice to the reporter remains in force.
- An objective record mismatch can block the affected action under existing lifecycle rules. In particular, final-invoice differences from the authorised quote plus effective variations block confirmation and notify MCST and admin. A suspicious pattern or configurable threshold breach is a warning for human review, not a finding of fraud or an automatic job pause. Admin may impose a reasoned, audited pause when proceeding could compromise evidence or cause avoidable loss.
- A compulsory relationship declaration at each quote or selection is not wanted. Specific confidential reports and existing evidence can still be reviewed.
- MCST may set four warning rules for its estate: (1) a contractor quote's percentage change up or down from its first quote for the same RFQ scope, (2) percentage price difference when the selected quote exceeds the lowest current valid quote, (3) percentage growth of the job total through proposed variations from the selected quote, and (4) fewer than an MCST-set number of valid comparable quotes at selection. The first three use percentages only; there is no additional SGD difference floor. The fourth is a count, not a price threshold. Exact defaults are not decided.
- MCST sets estate defaults during onboarding, can edit them later in MCST Settings, and can set parameters for a specific ticket only. An estate-settings edit applies from the next RFQ round, preserving the settings and alerts on existing rounds. Every change records the named actor, exact server date/time, estate or ticket scope, previous and new values, and effective point.
- Before **Save preferences** or **Save settings** for the estate, MCST must tick an unchecked acknowledgment immediately above the button: **“I understand these warning settings apply from the next RFQ round. Existing jobs will continue to use their previous warning settings.”** Saving is unavailable until the box is ticked. The acknowledgment, setting version and save date/time are audited.
- A ticket-only override saved after quotes have arrived applies only to later events on that ticket. Earlier warnings and decisions retain the settings in force when they occurred; saving cannot erase or recalculate them. A short reason is required if bidding has begun. Before saving, MCST must tick a separate unchecked acknowledgment immediately above the button: **“I understand these new warning settings apply only to future events on this ticket. Earlier warnings and decisions will keep the settings that applied at the time.”** The actor, reason, old/new values, acknowledgment and exact server save date/time are audited. The event's recorded time, rather than a delayed notification time, determines which version applies.
- Correct version comparisons, cumulative variation growth, one grouped evidence alert per decision, and a quick attributable MCST review are approved. For a below-threshold CM selection, the CM sees the caution before submission and MCST is notified immediately afterward; it remains advisory and creates no extra approval gate.
- The three early additions are approved for the watchdog plan: a fair-information timeline, a decision-to-final price bridge, and protected-access event review. These reuse the existing version, decision and access evidence; their detailed triggers and recipients still need design.
- Contractor payment-recipient/bank-detail change verification is outside the initial watchdog scope. QuoteFix does not pay the contractor; MCST records the completed transfer under the existing lifecycle rule. No assumption is made here that QuoteFix stores or validates bank details.
- Launch notifications remain in-app only. A warning is a suspicion or discrepancy, not proof of collusion, kickbacks, or other misconduct. There is no fraud score and no imported historical data.

## Approved optimisation of the four rules — design detail

| Proposal | Why it helps | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Version estate and ticket rule settings and show their change date/time. | Makes it possible to explain why a warning fired and prevents silent rewriting of history. | Estate edits affect the next RFQ round; ticket overrides affect later ticket events only. Both saves require the relevant acknowledgment checkbox. |
| Use one evidence card per job/decision, grouping related triggers. | An MCST reviewing a selection can see price revision, selected-price difference and quote count together instead of receiving three bells. | Keep each trigger and underlying evidence independently auditable. |
| Compare like with like. | A material RFQ change, altered exclusions or different bundle can justify a price shift. | Quote-revision percentage uses the first quote for the current material scope version. Preserve earlier versions and show scope differences separately. |
| Calculate cumulative variation growth from the selected quote. | Several small variations cannot evade the warning simply because each is below the percentage threshold. | This is an advisory warning in addition to existing approval thresholds and invoice reconciliation. |
| Trigger at the relevant decision. | Revision warning at resubmission; selected-price and quote-count warning at selection; variation-growth warning at proposal and review. | Below-threshold CM selection is not held for MCST approval. MCST Select and approve shows the caution in the same confirmation. Exact bell grouping remains a copy/detail choice. |
| Require a review disposition, not a long form. | MCST can mark Explained, Needs clarification, Escalated to admin, or Unresolved, with an optional note and evidence link. | Acknowledgment is not approval or a finding. An unresolved warning stays visible in the decision pack. |
| Show why the rule fired and what it cannot establish. | Helps MCST judge an innocent explanation. | Include exact before/after amounts, percentage calculation, scope/version, timing, contractor reason and material term differences. Never call the price gap a market-price estimate or proven saving. |
| Reuse the existing sourcing checkpoint. | The shared plan already alerts Admin at the fixed second-day checkpoint if fewer than three eligible contractor organisations have selectable quotes. | MCST's configured count warns at selection; do not create a duplicate Admin timer or make three quotes mandatory. |

## Additional watchdog features, prioritised

1. **Fair-information timeline (approved early addition).** Show material RFQ changes, published Q&A, affected contractors notified, and quote reconfirmations in one timeline. Flag a quote still based on an outdated scope through the existing selection rules. The next actor is CM to correct/publish/reconfirm; MCST reviews the result. A legitimate site discovery is an ordinary explanation. Preserve restricted questioner identity and proprietary information.
2. **Decision-to-final price bridge (approved early addition).** On one screen, trace first quote, current quote, selected quote, each authorised variation, final invoice and confirmed total. Reuse existing immutable versions and analytics. Show exactly where price changed and who approved it. This is evidence presentation, not another approval gate.
3. **Possible split-job review (later proposal).** When linked tickets concern the same asset/location and a close time period, show MCST their combined scope and total relative to the existing estate approval threshold. Separate repairs, different trades and staged work may be legitimate. Require human review; do not automatically merge tickets, infer intent or prevent publication. Data/linking rules and recipients need owner decisions.
4. **Bidder-independence check (later proposal).** Admin can inspect whether supposedly different bidding organisations share verified company identifiers or other verified onboarding facts. Shared contact details alone may have innocent explanations, such as a common office or coordinator. Keep the underlying identity evidence admin-only; alert MCST only to a verified competition concern with a permitted summary. Do not expose rival quotes or personal data.
5. **Protected-access event review (approved early addition).** Record access to sealed quote versions, masked identities, restricted originals and confidential cases. A denied or out-of-role access attempt is an objective security event for Admin, with MCST informed when its procurement may be affected. Ordinary authorised CM viewing is not itself a collusion signal; off-platform information sharing cannot be established from these logs alone. Exact recipients and event grouping remain to be designed.
6. **Confidential allegation handling (early).** Reuse Request Admin Support with a restricted case, evidence attachments, subject exclusion, named investigator, dated actions and reasoned outcome. Keep allegations separate from rule warnings and from reputation. A case can remain unresolved without becoming a negative label on the contractor or CM.
7. **Estate-level concentration view (later, when enough real jobs exist).** Show the proportion of an estate's jobs and spend going to each contractor, alongside job types, quote counts and reasons for selection. Use new QuoteFix records only. This is a review aid, not an automatic collusion alert or contractor score. Minimum data and suitable comparison windows need decisions.
8. **Same-actor oversight (later proposal).** When one named MCST user authors an RFQ and uses Select and approve on it, show that fact in the decision pack and an estate oversight view. This preserves the approved one-action flow and adds no second approver. Whether it should generate a separate warning needs an owner decision; the same actor may be acting entirely properly.

## Alert and case boundaries proposed for review

- **Record-control failure:** exact invalid version, mismatched invoice, or permission violation. Existing affected action is blocked or access denied; the authorised actor corrects it, with Admin/MCST notified as already decided where applicable.
- **MCST policy warning:** one of the four configured thresholds is met. The job remains actionable; MCST sees evidence and records its review or escalates. Do not automatically pause work or notify the subject as an accusation.
- **Confidential conduct report:** restricted support case. Admin triages, protects the reporter, checks evidence, and shares only a permitted summary with an uninvolved MCST user if needed. Status distinguishes reported, under review, substantiated, not substantiated and unresolved; criteria for these outcomes still need definition.

No new reminder cadence, resolution deadline, fraud score, external data source, bank verification, or automatic adverse reputation effect is approved here. Existing notification, sourcing, approval and invoice controls remain authoritative.

## Separate proposal: checking alert usefulness

This means an optional MCST-only summary of how often each rule fired and how its warnings were handled, such as “quote-revision rule: 12 warnings; 9 explained, 2 escalated, 1 unresolved.” It would help MCST decide whether its own percentage is too sensitive. It would not change thresholds automatically, rank contractors, or judge that a report was false. The owner has asked for clarification; this feature is not yet approved and may be deferred.

## Open decisions for the integrity discussion

1. Exact default percentage for quote revision, selected-price difference and cumulative variation growth; exact MCST quote-count default. The 30% quote-revision example is illustrative, not approved as a default.
2. Exact wording of the two approved save acknowledgments may be polished before implementation, but their position before Save, required unchecked checkbox, prospective effect and preservation of earlier jobs/warnings are confirmed. The ticket-only override reason is required once bidding has begun.
3. The three approved early additions need precise event/recipient rules. Which of the other proposed features belong in first release, and what evidence threshold should cause MCST versus Admin attention?
4. Confirm whether an MCST review disposition is required at every consequential decision or can remain pending while the job proceeds. No additional MCST approval gate is introduced for below-threshold CM selection.
5. Outcome vocabulary and evidence standard for a confidential allegation. A report alone is never a finding.
6. Whether to add the optional MCST-only alert-usefulness summary after real usage. It is explanatory analytics, not a fraud score or automatic tuning.
